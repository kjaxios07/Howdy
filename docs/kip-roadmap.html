The shape of it

Five layers, one box.

Everything runs on a single server behind Cloudflare. That is deliberate: one machine is cheap, easy to reason about, and enough for the first several thousand students. Splitting it up is a problem to have later.

The answer pipeline

Done

server/app/routers/chat.py

  • Rate limit, then clean the message
  • Block personal details before they go anywhere
  • Ask the model, search live if the answer can change
  • Strip any source that is not on the trusted list

Each check is its own function. Adding one is a line, not a rewrite.

Topics & question bank

Done

Fifteen topics and the researched questions behind them. Adding a topic is an edit to one JSON file — no code change.

Version-controlled, so your research is recoverable even if the server is not.

Phase 2 · building now

Accounts, and answers that stay true.

Two jobs: let a student come back to what Kip told them last week, and make sure what Kip says this week is still correct.

Sign up & sign in

Building

server/app/routers/auth.py · web/auth.html

  • Google only — no password to steal or reset
  • Account keyed to Google's permanent id, not the email
  • Session lives on the server, revocable instantly

One file. Adding Apple or email sign-in later is a second function beside it.

Building

Reads the same topic list as the landing page, so they never drift apart.

How answers get saved

Done

server/app/crypto.py · models.py

  • Encrypted before it reaches storage, with a key stored separately
  • Each student's data locked with their own key
  • Guest and incognito never create a record at all

Storage assumes it will be stolen one day. A stolen copy reads as noise.

Nightly re-check

Building

server/app/evolve.py

  • 03:00 every day, no one presses anything
  • Re-reads the source behind every changeable figure
  • Corrects what moved; flags what it cannot confirm

Runs in its own container so a slow pass never slows a student's question.

Gap → draft → approve

Building

server/app/gaps.py · evolve.py

  • Records what Kip answered badly — the topic only, never who asked
  • Drafts an answer overnight, with sources
  • You approve it with one command before it goes live

This is how the product gets better every day without anyone writing content.

Measured — 19 Aug 2026

A cached answer costs 0.43c. One with a live search costs 2.04c, and half of that is the flat search fee, not tokens. Three searches — our cap — is 5.06c. Prices verified against the pricing page, not recalled; python -m app.costs estimate reproduces it, and once there is traffic report replaces the estimate with real usage.

What this actually decides

$5 is comfortable — a subscriber would have to ask four times more than a heavy user before it stops paying. The number to watch is not the paid tier at all, it is the free one: free users have no ceiling, and at scale they are the whole bill. Two levers exist and both are already built — prompt caching (worth about 4x) and the three-search cap.

The long run

What breaks at 500,000.

Modelled, not guessed — python -m app.scale. The surprising part is where the cost isn't.

Servers are not the problem

At half a million users the whole thing still peaks at about 26 requests a second and fits on one or two boxes — infrastructure is $496 of a $66,000 bill, under 1%. The model bill is everything. Any plan that starts with "we'll need Kubernetes" is solving the wrong problem.

Quotas are the wrong tool for this

Per-user caps bound abuse — one person hammering the thing. They do nothing about the actual bill, because that comes from normal use multiplied by a lot of people. Half a million students behaving perfectly still costs $66k a month. The fix has to be structural.

Who pays decides everything

With the levers in, $14.2k of cost against $50k of consumer revenue works. But the honest version is that the $5 tier alone never funds 500,000 free users — it funds about 10,000 subscribers. The free tier is paid for by institutions: at $4 per enrolled international student per year, 25% coverage adds $42k a month and the whole thing is comfortable. That is why the university deal is Phase 3 and not an afterthought.

Why the paid tier is not a discount list

UNiDAYS and Student Beans are free, and better resourced at aggregating offers than we will ever be. Charging for that is a losing position at $5 or $10. What a student cannot get anywhere else — and would genuinely pay for — is not missing a deadline: visa expiry, OSHC lapse, lease end, tax. Missing one is the highest-stakes thing in their year here, and tracking it costs us a scheduled job and a notification, with no model call at all.

Do not raise the price first

$5 at 2% conversion and $10 at 1% are identical revenue. Doubling the price with the same features drops conversion further than the price rises — the config carries a live: false flag so $10 cannot go out before the keeper does. Margin is not the constraint either way: at realistic use a subscriber costs $1.61/month, so $5 is 68% gross and $10 is 84%. The constraint is willingness to pay, and that is a feature problem.

Three things not to do

Never put advertising inside an answer — it is the fastest money and it ends the university business permanently. Never charge for emergency or safety help. Never take a commission per referral, only flat fees, so nobody can ever ask whether Kip sent them somewhere for the money.
