The line we don't cross

Kip knows the edge of its own competence.

Under the Migration Act 1958, giving immigration assistance without registration is a criminal offence. Giving general information is not. That distinction isn't a disclaimer at the bottom of a page — it's built into every question in the bank.

Privacy

You can ask about a visa refusal without it existing anywhere.

People ask us things they haven't told their family. So the system was designed on one assumption: one day someone will get a copy of the database. The job is to make that copy worthless.

Signed out

Guest

Full access to Kip. No account, and nothing written to the database at all. Close the tab and it is genuinely gone — there's no delete step that could quietly fail, because no row is ever created.

Nothing stored
Signed in, opted out

Incognito

Your history from earlier is there, but this conversation isn't written. One toggle in the chat window, on and off mid-conversation.

Per-conversation
Signed in, default

Saved

Conversations kept so Kip remembers the thread of your arrival — encrypted before they reach the database, with a key the database doesn't contain. You pick 30 days, 90 days, a year, or until you delete them.

AES-256-GCM
What a stolen database contains

Random bytes

Every message, every conversation title and every email address is encrypted before it's written. The per-user keys in the database are themselves encrypted by a master key that lives on a different part of the server — one the database process can't read, and that is never in a backup.

No password database exists to steal. Sign-in is Google, and we never see one.

What we never collect

The list is short on purpose

No date of birth. No address. No phone. No visa or passport number. If you paste a tax file number, Medicare number or card number into the chat, it's detected and the message is neither stored nor sent to the model — Kip tells you not to share it and carries on.

Raw IP addresses are never written down, anywhere.

Straight answers

The questions people ask before they trust it.

Is it actually free?

Yes, and the safety content stays free permanently — emergencies, scams, exploitation, mental health. Charging a frightened student to find out who to call would be indefensible.

Universities pay to run a co-branded version for their cohort. That's the business, not you.

Is this legal or migration advice?

No, and it legally cannot be. Under the Migration Act 1958 only a MARA-registered agent or an Australian legal practitioner may advise on your own immigration circumstances.

Kip gives general information: what a visa is, what it costs, what it requires, how the process runs. The moment a question turns on your situation, it says so and names who to go to.

Do you save my conversations?

Only if you're signed in and haven't turned it off. Signed out, nothing is written to the database at all — the code never builds a row, so there's no cleanup step that could quietly fail.

Signed in, everything you type is encrypted before it reaches the database, and you choose how long it's kept. See Privacy.

If I ask about my employer, could it affect my visa?

Not through us — we don't know who you are and don't report to anyone.

More importantly: the Fair Work Ombudsman has an arrangement with Home Affairs so that visa holders who report exploitation are protected. That protection exists precisely so you can speak up. Kip will explain it and give you the number: 13 13 94, free, with interpreters.

What happens when Kip doesn't know?

It says so and names the official site to check, rather than producing a confident guess. That's a rule in the system prompt, not a hope.

The question also gets logged as a coverage gap — the topic only, with no link to you and no timestamp — and worked on. That's how the answer bank grows.

Which languages does it speak?

Ask in your own language and Kip answers in it. The cited sources stay in English because that's what the official pages are, and you should be able to open and check them.

For anything urgent, the crisis lines in the footer all provide free interpreters — you never have to explain yourself in English.

Where is my data physically stored?

On a server we control, not a third-party AI platform. For institutional deployments that server is in Sydney, because Australian data residency is what university procurement asks about first.

Anthropic's API sees your question and the recent conversation — never your name, email or account. It cannot identify you.

Can my university have this?

Yes — co-branded, with your orientation dates, accommodation office, student legal service and emergency contacts loaded in, plus a dashboard showing what your cohort is struggling with this month.
